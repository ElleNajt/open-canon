#+TITLE: Evolving Music with LLMs: A Comparative Study
#+DATE: 2026-02-17

* The Experiment

We give four frontier LLMs the same seed -- a piece of music written in
Strudel (a live-coding language for music) -- and ask them to repeatedly
"evolve" it. Each model runs independently for 30 steps, receiving its
own previous output each time. The prompt is minimal:

#+begin_quote
Evolve this according to your preferences
#+end_quote

The system prompt is three lines: return only code, no markdown fences,
keep it under 500 lines. No style guidance, no music theory, no
examples. We want to see what each model's aesthetic instincts produce.

** Models

- Claude Sonnet 4.6 (Anthropic API, direct)
- GPT-4.1 (OpenRouter)
- Gemini 2.5 Pro (OpenRouter)
- Grok 4.1 Fast (OpenRouter)

** Seeds

- Bach BWV 1087 Canon 3 (two-voice canon with bass)
- Bach BWV 1087 Canon 9 (two-voice canon with bass)
- Twinkle Twinkle Little Star (simple melody, triangle wave)

* Observations

** The Grok Collapse

Grok has a distinctive failure mode: it periodically deletes everything.
A piece will grow normally over several steps (1162 -> 1693 -> 1961
chars), then suddenly collapse to near-zero (40 chars -- just a
comment). This happened consistently across multiple seeds and runs.

Example from Bach Canon 3, step 10:
#+begin_src js
// BWV 1087 Canon 4 Hyper-Evolved v6

//
#+end_src

That's the entire output. The previous step was nearly 2000 characters
of working music.

Same pattern in Canon 9: steps 0-3 grew normally, step 4 collapsed to 5
lines (just a voice definition, no notes).

This appears to be a Grok-specific behavior -- the other models never
exhibited it. We added a guard: if the output is less than 20% of the
input length, we reject it and keep the previous code.

** The Complexity Explosion

All models share a tendency to make the music increasingly complex with
each step. What starts as a simple melody quickly becomes a dense wall
of layered tracks, effects chains, and modulations. By step 15-20, a
simple Twinkle Twinkle has become a 10-track orchestral piece with FM
synthesis, ring modulation, shabda speech samples, and euclidean
rhythms.

Claude is the worst offender -- producing 15-20k character outputs per
step (compared to GPT's 5-10k and Grok's 2-5k). More isn't better:
the resulting music is often an overwhelming wash of sound where no
individual voice is discernible.

This is the "more is more" instinct that all LLMs seem to share when
given creative freedom. Left unchecked, they treat "evolve" as "add
more stuff" rather than "refine what's there." We added a prompt
instruction to prefer clarity over complexity.

** What Makes It Sound Inhuman

Even when the code is syntactically valid and the piece "works," the
outputs share patterns that no human musician would produce:

*** Everything modulates, nothing is anchored

By step 30, every parameter on every track is attached to a
=sine.range().slow()=. Filter cutoff, reverb amount, FM index, ring
mod depth, detune, phaser rate, panning -- all drifting simultaneously.
A human musician picks one or two things to move and leaves the rest as
a stable foundation. When everything moves, nothing moves: the ear has
no contrast to latch onto.

*** Effect stacking without identity

A single track in Claude's Canon 3 output chains 18 effect parameters:
vowel filter, detune, FM (3 params), pitch envelope (2 params),
tremolo (3 params), phaser (3 params), reverb (3 params), and a juxBy
layer. No human would apply every effect at moderate amounts. A good
synth patch picks 2-3 defining effects and commits to them -- "this is
the phaser voice," not "this is the everything voice."

*** No dynamics or structure

These pieces are continuous textures with no arc. No verse/chorus
thinking, no drops, no call-and-response between tracks. The =.every()=
conditionals in some outputs are the closest thing to structural
variation, but they're cosmetic -- they don't create sections or
tension/release.

*** The prime number fetish

Claude especially loves =.slow(113)=, =.slow(127)=, =.slow(167)= --
all primes. The idea (presumably) is that coprime modulation rates
never sync up, creating "organic" movement. In practice it means
nothing ever resolves or arrives anywhere. Human music has moments of
alignment: the downbeat, the cadence, the resolution.

*** Reverb as default, not choice

Almost every track across all models ends up with =.room(0.88+)= and
=.size(0.97+)=. Everything swims in the same vast reverb. A human mix
uses dry/wet contrast to create depth -- close present elements against
distant washed ones. When everything is equally wet, the spatial
dimension collapses.

*** Inaudible ghost layers

Several models produce =.off()= and =.jux()= layers with gains like
0.008, 0.011, 0.018. These are functionally silent. It appears to be
an artifact of gradual reduction: each step makes a layer "a little
more subtle" until it's below the threshold of hearing but never gets
deleted.

We addressed these with prompt constraints: max 5-6 effects per track,
anchor most parameters and modulate only 1-2 per track, vary reverb
across tracks, minimum gain of 0.05, and filter Q below 10.
